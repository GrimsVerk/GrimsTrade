# gap-2002-1: how far does `tsmom12` trail holding since 2002, per series?

Lane gap-2002-1, 2026-11-24 (night). Branch `gap-2002-1`. Research only: fake money, files
already in the data cache, no change to the gym, the daily runner or the live strategies. A
descriptive re-score of finished tries (insure-2, insure-3) with their own code: no new rule,
nothing selected. Train data only: nothing after 2025-10-31 is parsed (both loaders stop there).

## Why

`SUMMARY.md` ("But it has a price", amended in #160) says the rule trails holding since 2002 by
"about 2 points a year as the mean over the US market and 10 industries (insure-2), about
0.7-1.1 on the S&P 500 total return (insure-3)". The two numbers use different series, cash
conventions and measures (insure-2: mean log points per series-year, cash at 0, episodes
counted by peak; insure-3: CAGR points, bills and 0). This lane puts them on one footing per
series, so the sentence can say what the broad index costs and what the industries cost.

## Method (fixed before any number is computed)

Written 2026-11-24 ~21:40 UTC, before the script was run. What had been seen: the published
tables of insure-2 (step 1 detail: 2002-2025 sums I +1703, B -1784, C -492, -2.19 log points
per series-year, pooled over the 11 series, cash at 0) and insure-3 (main and side tables, and
its note "0.7 (bills) to 1.0-1.1 (cash 0) points a year since 2002"). No per-series number for
2002-2025 had been seen.

**Series.**

- The French daily US market and the 10 value-weighted industries, exactly insure-2 step 1's
  inputs: `insure1.build` on the cached French snapshot (2026-11-12), the rule as its paper
  series (`trend_tune.paper`, Friday decisions, 2 bps a side), holding = the total-return
  index. Bills = the French daily RF column (insure-1's T-bill reading). The saved gym runs are
  not needed: insure-2 step 1 uses the paper series only.
- The S&P 500 total return, exactly insure-3's series: `insure3.load()` (`hold`, `trend` with
  idle cash at bills from `^IRX`, `trend@cash0`).

**Window.** Returns of sessions dated 2002-01-01 .. 2025-10-31. Years Y = (2025-10-31 -
2002-01-01) / 365.25 (insure-2's divisor).

**Gap.** G = 100 x sum over window sessions of [ln(1 + r_rule) - ln(1 + r_hold)] / Y: rule
minus holding, log points a year (negative = the rule trails). Two cash conventions: 0
(r_rule = paper return) and bills (r_rule = paper return + (1 - position) x bill return, as
insure-1's `d_tb` and insure-3's `trend`). Also shown: the CAGR gap in simple points a year
(CAGR hold - CAGR rule, insure-3's measure), so the S&P row can be matched to insure-3's 0.7-1.1.

**Split (insure-1/insure-2 definitions).** On each series' own total-return index over its
whole loaded history (French: from 1927-07-02; S&P: insure-3's days from 1986-06-30): zigzag
25% down / 25% up; fall = sessions (peak, trough]; rebound = (trough, the earlier of trough +
365 days and the next peak]; calm = every other session. Per series and cash convention:

- **Fall gain I**, **rebound lag B**, **calm drag C**, each summed over window sessions and
  divided by Y, **by date**: every window session counts in the part it falls in, so
  I + B + C = G exactly. This is the primary reading.
- **By peak (insure-2's accounting)**, as a reconciliation: the window's episodes are those
  whose peak lies in it, their I and B counted in full; C = calm sessions in the window. Its
  pooled 11-series total at cash 0 must reproduce insure-2's -2.19 (check; a mismatch is
  reported, not fixed silently). The difference from the by-date reading is the part of the
  2000-2002 falls and rebounds that lies after 2002-01-01 but belongs to episodes that peaked
  before.

**Mean.** Equal-weight mean over the 11 French series (insure-2's population), and the same
over the 10 industries alone; the S&P 500 is shown on its own (it is the same market as the
French market series, differently built: shown side by side, not averaged in).

**Expectation, stated so it can be wrong.** The French market alone trails by about 1-1.5 log
points a year at cash 0 and less with bills, close to insure-3's S&P figure; the industries
carry the 11-series mean up to about 2. By date, the rebound lag is larger than the calm drag
in most series.

## Output and rows

- `lib/gap2002.py` writes `results/gap-2002-1.md`: one table per series (G, I, B, C by date
  and by peak, both cash conventions, CAGR gap), the means, and the reconciliation check.
- One `tries.md` row of kind `note` (a re-score of logged tries, not a new try).
- Bar for scale only (BAR.md): N 500, z >= 4.70, Bonferroni p <= 1.0e-04 for a new pick. Not
  applicable: a description, no test, no pick.
- The corrected sentence for SUMMARY's "But it has a price" bullet goes into `SUMMARY.md` in
  this PR, from these numbers.
