# Literature survey, November 2026

Question: for a daily-bar, long-only, unlevered book in six assets (BTC, ETH, SPY, QQQ, GLD, TLT),
filled at the next open and paying about 60 bps per crypto round trip and 4 bps per ETF round
trip, what has held up out of sample after costs, and what failed?

How it was made: six reading passes, one per family, each writing a file in
`notes/survey-parts/` with one entry per source. Every entry there is tagged with how far the
source was actually read (`full text`, `abstract page`, `secondary summary`, `not opened`,
`from memory, unverified`); numbers are quoted only as the source states them. This file is the
compilation: a verdict per family, then every source with its link. About 150 sources were
opened, roughly a third in full. Where our own run 1 tested the idea, the last column says what
we found (details in `results/`).

## Verdict per family

| Family | What the literature says | Verdict for our setup | Our own test (run 1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trend following, time-series momentum (part 1) | Real across 50-67 futures markets and every decade since 1880; did its job in 2008 and 2022. But much of the profit is shorts, leverage and volatility scaling; without scaling it is about buy-and-hold. Post-publication record is poor: Faber's filter 4.95% a year against 11.64% for buy-and-hold in 2009-12; SG Trend Index Sharpe 0.05 in 2010-18 | **Holds up as a loss reducer, not as a return booster.** Slow signals (10-12 months) beat fast ones after costs | Agrees. `tsmom12` 1.05 and `sma200` 1.01 Sharpe against 0.88 for equal weight (p = 0.08 and 0.17); drawdown about halved; weaker on other markets |

## What this means for the lab

1. **Protection carries over, return does not.** Across families the robust findings are about
   risk (volatility, drawdown), not about earning more. That matches our own tests.
2. **Slow beats fast after costs,** most of all in crypto.
3. **Every exotic indicator has a plain twin** (rolling variance, mean correlation, price over
   moving average). In run 1 the twin won or tied every time.
4. **Published signals decay.** Two for two in our post-publication tests.
5. **Testing rules adopted** (in `protocol.md`): hypothesis and parameters fixed first; few
   variants; every try logged; plain twin required; out-of-universe check; per-era reporting; one
   look at a held-out year; the forward paper ledger is the only real out-of-sample test.

## Gaps and cautions

- Many sources were read only as abstracts; SSRN, some publishers and Alpha Architect blocked
  automated reading. The part files mark each one. Numbers from abstracts are not cross-checked.
- Almost all papers fill at the close and many are long-short and levered. "Verdict for our
  setup" is our inference, not the authors' claim.
- Not covered: macro signals (yield curve, credit spreads), options-implied measures, and
  sentiment data. They need data the keyless sources do not give.
