# Holding physical gold yourself in the Netherlands: costs, cash rules, storage, outages

Research note, written 2026-11-24. Companion to `research/exit-plan-2026/report.md` (section 7,
parking).

The scenario: a euro-based retail investor living in the Netherlands wants to hold part of a small
portfolio as physical gold that they can reach themselves, so that some assets stay reachable
during bank or IT outages. The note covers what such gold costs at Dutch dealers, the cash-payment
rules, tax, storage, insurance, fakes, selling back, the general sizing ranges that are published,
the official advice for outages, and what real bank outages looked like.

This is research help, not financial advice. Dealer prices are a snapshot of Tue 24 Nov 2026,
about 11:30-12:15 CET, read from dealers' public price lists; no dealer was contacted. "UNVERIFIED"
marks anything no source in this run confirmed.

## 1. Summary

- **Premiums over spot** (spot about EUR 3,316 per troy ounce; dealers' own published prices):
  1 oz bullion coins (Krugerrand, Maple Leaf, Philharmoniker) **+2.5% to +7.4%**; 1 oz bars
  **+3.9% to +5.2%**; 10 g bars **+7.8% to +9.3%**; 1/10 oz coins **+19% to +24%**. Some walk-in
  shops add a surcharge for over-the-counter sales.
- **Buy-back** (where published): 1 oz coins from **at spot to 2% under spot**; bars **1.5-4% under
  spot**. Round trip (buy, then sell back) on a 1 oz coin: about **2.5% to 9%** depending on dealer
  and channel. Some walk-in buyers publish no prices and make an offer after inspection.
- **Most dealers keep no stock on site.** Most offices checked work by appointment with stock in an
  external vault (pickup 1-2 days after ordering); walk-in shops where you can take coins home at
  once exist but carry a limited, changing range.
- **Cash:** since **1 Jan 2026** Dutch traders in goods may not pay or accept **EUR 3,000 or more in
  cash** (Wwft art. 1f), linked purchases added up, in both directions (buying and selling back).
  Dealers' cash caps are EUR 2,990-3,000 (some per month); one adds a 2% cash fee; one large
  dealer stopped taking cash altogether. The Wwft now puts no ID duty on goods traders, but dealers
  ask ID anyway, and VAT rules make them record identity for investment-gold deals above EUR 10,000.
  An **EU cap of EUR 10,000** applies from **10 July 2027** (AML Regulation art. 80); lower national
  limits such as the Dutch one stay.
- **Tax:** investment gold is **VAT-exempt** (silver: 21%; a 1 oz silver coin cost +18% over spot at
  one dealer, against +3.3% for the gold coin of the same series). **Box 3:** gold is "overige
  bezittingen", deemed return **6.00% for 2026** (final), taxed at 36%: about **2.2% of the
  1 January value per year** on wealth above the allowance (EUR 59,357 per person in 2026).
- **Storage:** home-contents policies often **exclude** bullion or cap it at **EUR 500-7,500**
  (one insurer's top policy: EUR 200,000 only in a certified, anchored safe). One large bank is
  ending safe-deposit boxes, and bank boxes are reachable only when the branch is open. Dealer vaults cost about
  0.5% a year, but access runs through the dealer's systems.
- **Sizing, published general practice (not advice):** World Gold Council research 2-10% (about 5%
  base case); Dalio "All Weather" 7.5%; Browne "Permanent Portfolio" 25%; Dutch wealth planners
  5-10%.
- **For outages, official advice is cash, not gold:** DNB advises **EUR 70 per adult and EUR 30 per
  child** at home for 72 hours; the government's Denk vooruit kit lists cash. Real outages mostly
  lasted **hours** (Dutch card outages 2018 and 2024: 1.5-2 hours), some **days** (Barclays 2025:
  about 3), one migration **months** (TSB 2018). Gold cannot be spent in a shop and selling it needs
  an open dealer and pays under EUR 3,000 in cash; it addresses a long, deep failure of the banking
  system rather than the outages actually seen.

## 2. Dealer prices and terms (generic)

Six Dutch dealers' public pages were read (national online dealers with offices, a walk-in shop, a
walk-in chain). Premium = price / spot value of the gold content - 1, against the spot the same
dealer showed (EUR 106,574-106,724 per kg in the window).

| Product | Premium range seen | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 oz bullion coin, new | +2.5% to +7.4% | Lowest at national online dealers; highest at a dealer with a cash fee and pickup fee |
| 1 oz bullion coin, bought over the counter in a walk-in shop | a few % above the same dealer's online price | Over-the-counter surcharge |
| 1 oz coin, used ("gecirculeerd") | about +3.3% | Longer delivery |
| 1 oz (31.1 g) bar (Umicore, Heraeus) | +3.9% to +5.2% | |
| 10 g bar | +7.8% to +9.3% | |
| 1/10 oz coin | +19% to +24% | Premium per gram about 5-7 times the 1 oz coin's |

| Buy-back (published lists) | Range |
|---|---|
| 1 oz coin | at spot to 2% under spot |
| 1/10 oz coin | about 0.5% under spot (one dealer) |
| 10 g and 1 oz bars | 1.5% to 4% under spot |

Terms seen:
- Appointment: national dealers' offices are by appointment only, with stock in external vaults;
  ordering at an office does not mean taking the gold home that day. Pickup fees up to EUR 25.
- Walk-in: a few shops sell from limited stock without appointment, sometimes with a small
  over-the-counter surcharge on top of the webshop price and a minimum purchase. A
  walk-in chain holds a small selection for direct cash or anonymous sales; other items are ordered
  and collected.
- Cash: caps of EUR 2,990-3,000 (some per person per month, some only with name recorded; one adds
  a 2% cash fee); one dealer accepts no cash since 1 Jan 2026 and takes a debit card at
  the counter. Several refuse EUR 200/500 notes.
- ID: shown at office visits even for "anonymous" cash buys (visitor registers); always required
  when selling back.

## 3. Cash-payment law

- **Wwft art. 1f (in force 1 Jan 2026):** "Het is verboden voor instellingen als bedoeld in artikel
  1a, vierde lid, onderdelen i, k en n, in of vanuit Nederland betaling van goederen in contanten
  voor een bedrag van EUR 3.000 of meer ... te verrichten of te accepteren, ongeacht of de
  transactie plaatsvindt in een handeling of door middel van meer handelingen waartussen een verband
  lijkt te bestaan." Category i is professional buyers and sellers of goods (a bullion dealer trading
  for its own account falls here; the Wwft has no separate gold-dealer category); k art dealers;
  n pawnshops. ([consolidated text, BWB](https://repository.officiele-overheidspublicaties.nl/bwb/BWBR0024282/2026-07-01_0/xml/BWBR0024282_2026-07-01_0.xml);
  [wetten.overheid.nl](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0024282/))
  - Belastingdienst: cash "tot en met EUR 2.999,99" stays allowed
    ([Belastingdienst](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/themaoverstijgend/brochures_en_publicaties/verbod-contante-betalingen-3000)).
  - Rijksoverheid: only transactions with or between traders in goods; no sector exceptions;
    private sales are not covered
    ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/economie/financiele-sector/verbod-contante-betalingen-van-3000-euro)).
  - Bill 36228 "Wet plan van aanpak witwassen": submitted Oct 2022; three of four measures dropped
    June 2024; Tweede Kamer 24 Sep 2024; Eerste Kamer 10 Jun 2025; Stb. 2025, 262 and 362
    ([Eerste Kamer](https://www.eerstekamer.nl/wetsvoorstel/36228_wet_plan_van_aanpak)).
- **Identification:** until 31 Dec 2025, goods traders fell under the Wwft only for cash payments of
  EUR 10,000 or more. Since 1 Jan 2026, Wwft art. 1a lid 1 leaves art. 1f as their only Wwft duty:
  no Wwft customer due diligence and no Wwft reporting duty. Stb. 2026, 99 (in force 30 Apr 2026)
  removed the cash reporting indicators: EUR 10,000 for goods traders, EUR 20,000 for intermediaries
  in goods incl. precious metals, art dealers and pawnshops
  ([Stb. 2026, 99](https://repository.officiele-overheidspublicaties.nl/officielepublicaties/stb/2026/stb-2026-99/1/xml/stb-2026-99.xml)).
  No EUR 2,000 threshold for gold exists in Dutch law. Intermediaries in precious metals (art. 1a
  lid 4 j) keep full Wwft duties, without a cash indicator.
  - VAT rule: dealers record each investment-gold transaction above **EUR 10,000** with proof of the
    customer's identity
    ([Belastingdienst](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/btw/tarieven_en_vrijstellingen/vrijstellingen/beleggingsgoud/)).
- **EU AML Regulation (EU) 2024/1624** ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1624/oj)):
  art. 80(1) caps cash payments for goods and services at **EUR 10,000**; art. 80(2)-(3) let lower
  national limits continue; art. 80(7) lets a member state **suspend the cap temporarily** if
  non-cash payment becomes unavailable nationally through force majeure; applies from **10 July
  2027** (art. 90). Precious-metal traders become obliged entities whatever the payment method
  (art. 3(3)(e)), with customer due diligence for occasional transactions of EUR 10,000 or more
  (art. 19(1)(b)); when a dealer buys, the seller counts as its customer (art. 19(6)(a)); art. 19(9)
  lets lower thresholds be set for higher-risk sectors (none known yet).
- **Selling back:** Sr art. 437 lid 1(b) forbids a designated trader to acquire a used good without
  recording the seller's identifying details; the implementing decree names metals and precious
  stones; many municipalities require the police's Digitaal Opkopers Register. Whether a sealed
  investment bar is a "used good" is UNVERIFIED; dealers ask ID on every buy-back anyway. Cash
  payouts are capped below EUR 3,000 too (one dealer: at most EUR 2,950); the rest goes by bank
  transfer.

## 4. VAT, box 3, storage, fakes, selling back

### 4.1 VAT

Investment gold is VAT-exempt
([Belastingdienst](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/btw/tarieven_en_vrijstellingen/vrijstellingen/beleggingsgoud/wat_is_beleggingsgoud)):
bars in market-accepted weights with purity at least 995/1000; coins at least 900/1000 pure, minted
after 1800, legal tender (now or before) in the country of origin, normally sold at no more than 80%
above their gold value. Coins on the EU's annual list always qualify (for 2026: OJ C/2025/5923;
contents not read, UNVERIFIED). EU basis: VAT Directive 2006/112/EC arts 344-356. Jewellery gold is
taxed. Silver is outside the scheme: 21% VAT, or the margin scheme for some coins; a new 1 oz silver
Maple Leaf cost +18.2% over spot at one dealer (VAT "marge of 21%" included), against +3.3% for the
1 oz gold Maple Leaf at the same dealer.

### 4.2 Box 3

- Movable things held as an investment are **overige bezittingen**, valued at market value on
  1 January ([Belastingdienst fisin2026](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/fisin/fisin2026/bezittingen_en_schulden_box_3_);
  the page does not name gold). For gold coins, SRA (24 Nov 2025) reports that the Belastingdienst,
  when asked, treats them as overige bezittingen even when legal tender, not as cash
  ([SRA](https://www.sra.nl/nieuws/004501/2025/11/hoog-forfaitair-rendement-voor-gouden-munten-in-box-3);
  the same page wrongly gives 7.78% for 2026).
- 2026: deemed return **6.00%** (final), rate 36%, allowance **EUR 59,357** per person; 2025:
  5.88% and EUR 57,684
  ([Belastingdienst 2026](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/box-3/content/berekening-box-3-inkomen-2026);
  Wet IB 2001 art. 5.2, 2026 version,
  [BWB](https://repository.officiele-overheidspublicaties.nl/bwb/BWBR0011353/2026-01-01_0/xml/BWBR0011353_2026-01-01_0.xml)).
  Rough cost 36% x 6% = 2.16% of value per year, on box 3 wealth above the allowance.
- The actual-return route (tegenbewijsregeling; in the tax return from 2025) counts unrealised gains and
  gives no allowance, so it helps only in flat or falling years
  ([Belastingdienst](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/nl/box-3/content/wat-is-mijn-werkelijk-rendement)).
- The planned actual-return law aims at "uiterlijk 1 januari 2029" (Eerste Kamer pending, amended
  by a novelle in autumn 2026); how it will tax physical gold is UNVERIFIED
  ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/inkomstenbelasting/plannen-werkelijk-rendement-box-3)).

### 4.3 Storage and insurance

- **At home:** bullion ("onbewerkt edelmetaal") is often excluded or capped low in home-contents
  policies:
  - Avéro Achmea Inboedel Top (July 2025): edelmetaal including bullion coins EUR 7,500 theft cap
    outside a safe; EUR 200,000 in a safe of EN 1143-1 Grade II (or value rating at least
    EUR 23,000), anchored if under 1,000 kg
    ([policy PDF](https://averoachmea.nl/-/media/files/zakelijk/voorwaarden-poz/voorwaarden-2025/voorwaarden-inboedel-top---inb-bv-50-251.pdf)).
  - ASN Inboedel: cash, securities, loose gems and raw precious metal together EUR 500
    ([policy](https://asnbank.nl/downloads/voorwaarden-asn-inboedelverzekering-1.html)).
  - ZLM (2026-27) and Promovendum (2025): raw precious metal excluded.
- **Bank safe-deposit box:** Rabobank is ending safe-deposit boxes at all offices
  ([Rabobank](https://www.rabobank.nl/particulieren/service/beeindiging-safeloketten), seen in a
  search index only); other banks' 2026 offer UNVERIFIED. Bank cover per box was EUR 25,000-50,000
  in 2018 (regional press), with proof of contents. Access only during branch opening hours: for the outage purpose,
  a bank box depends on the bank being open and working. Private box companies (from about
  EUR 12 a month, [ANWB](https://www.anwb.nl/verzekeringen/ledenvoordeel/de-nederlandse-kluis))
  replace them.
- **Dealer vault:** about 0.5% a year for gold, insured, AFM-licensed and bankruptcy-remote per the
  dealers' own sites ([example](https://www.goldrepublic.com/nl-nl/opslag)); ownership is usually
  grams of larger bars. Access runs through the dealer's website and systems.

### 4.4 Fakes

No Dutch statistics on fake bullion were found (UNVERIFIED how common). The classic fake is
gold-plated tungsten (density 19.3 vs 19.32 g/cm3), which passes weight and size checks
([dealer explainer](https://www.hollandgold.nl/100-vragen-over-goud/kun-je-goud-vervalsen-met-wolfraam-tungsten/)).
Established dealers buy from mints and LBMA-accredited refiners and sell small bars sealed with a
serial number and certificate. Keeping the packaging matters at resale: dealers pay less for
non-LBMA, damaged or unpacked bars and may refuse self-melted ones. Private sales add fake and
stolen-goods risk and no buy-back.

### 4.5 Selling back

Buyers are the dealer you bought from (some buy back only their own metal), national dealers with
published buy-back prices, and walk-in buyers who make an offer after inspection. ID is required.
BNNVARA Kassa / NOS (24 Oct 2025) found a walk-in gold buyer paying less-informed sellers 20-30% of
the value of jewellery ([NOS](https://nos.nl/l/2587780)); that was jewellery, not sealed bullion,
but it shows why a published buy-back price matters.

## 6. Outages: official advice and what happened

**Official advice:**
- DNB / MOB, 20 May 2025: prepare for 72 hours without card payments; keep "rond de EUR 70 per
  volwassene en EUR 30 per kind" in mixed notes and coins, plus a physical debit card and more than
  one way to pay
  ([DNB](https://www.dnb.nl/algemeen-nieuws/persbericht-2025/advies-mob-bereid-je-voor-op-drie-dagen-uitval-pinnen/)).
  A DNB survey (25 Feb 2026) found 71% knew the advice and 58% had the cash at home
  ([DNB](https://www.dnb.nl/algemeen-nieuws/nieuws-2026/advies-over-contant-geld-voor-nood-bij-meerderheid-consumenten-bekend/)).
- Rijksoverheid: same amounts; no legal limit on cash at home
  ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/recht-veiligheid-en-defensie/dreiging-in-nederland/vraag-en-antwoord/betalingsverkeer/contant-geld-thuis-bewaren)).
- Denk vooruit (NCTV), launched 1-3 Nov 2025: prepare for "72 uur zonder water, stroom of
  internet"; the kit list includes "Contant geld", no amount
  ([denkvooruit.nl](https://www.denkvooruit.nl/bereid-je-voor/stel-je-noodpakket-samen)). No
  official Dutch source mentions gold.
- EU Preparedness Union Strategy, 26 Mar 2025: 72-hour self-sufficiency
  ([Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/preparedness-union_en)).

**What the evidence supports:** outages seen so far lasted hours to a few days. For those, cash
already at home works; gold does not (no shop takes it, and selling needs an open dealer, ID, and
pays under EUR 3,000 in cash, the rest by bank transfer). Gold addresses a rarer case: a long, deep
failure where balances are frozen for weeks or more, or high inflation. Its costs for that
insurance: a 2.5-9% round trip (20%+ on small coins), about 2.2% a year in box 3 above the
allowance, an insurance gap at home, and price swings (gold fell 16% in euros in the Lehman weeks,
exit-plan report section 7.1).
