# Lane `park`: where to park the cash, and what selling costs

Lane of the exit-plan-2026 run (night lead, 2026-11-18). Research only: public pages, no accounts,
no quotes needing personal data. Every number carries the date it was read (or the source's own
date) and a link. "(unverified)" marks what no fetched page confirmed. Not tax research. Historical
crash behaviour of havens is the `haven` lane; this lane covers products, guarantees, costs and
logistics for a euro investor in the Netherlands with a small portfolio (a few thousand euros).

All pages read 2026-11-18 unless another date is given.

## 0. Bottom line (for the lead)

- **Latest safe fallback sale date for shares: Monday 21 Dec 2026** (trade date on Oslo Børs or
  Euronext). It settles Wed 23 Dec, leaving Mon 28 - Thu 31 Dec (four bank days) for the broker
  payout, any currency conversion and the transfer to a euro account. Better: **Fri 18 Dec**
  (settles Tue 22 Dec), which adds 23 Dec as a spare day. Hard limits: the last sale that certainly
  settles in 2026 is **Mon 28 Dec** (settles Wed 30 Dec); the last Oslo trading day of 2026 is
  **Wed 30 Dec**, but that trade settles only on **Mon 4 Jan 2027**. Details in section 4.
  **These dates are for shares.**
- **Funds (review round 1, section 7):** fund sales do not settle T+2. Typical platform figures are
  2-5 business days (Nordic funds) or 2-6 (other funds) from the order to cash, and fund companies
  often do not deal on 24, 25 or 31 Dec (global funds also not 28 Dec). Recommended fallback for
  funds: **Fri 11 Dec**; last safe: **Wed 16 Dec** (2-5 days) / **Tue 15 Dec** (2-6 days); on a
  slow platform (5-10 days) Fri 4 Dec / Fri 11 Dec.
- **Crash-safe parking for weeks to months, best supported (3-5 options):**
  1. Instant-access savings account under the Dutch (or another EU) deposit guarantee: zero market
     risk, EUR 100k cover, payout within 7 working days (proven with Amsterdam Trade Bank, 2022).
     Rates now 1.10-1.52% at the big three, 3.11% promo at bunq (section 2).
  2. Euro overnight-rate ETF (Xtrackers XEON or Amundi CSH, TER 0.10%) or the iShares EUR Govt
     Bond 0-1yr ETF (IEGE on Euronext Amsterdam, TER 0.07%): earns about the ECB rate (2.50%),
     lives in the same broker account you will use to buy back in, sells in seconds and settles T+2.
  3. Broker cash at the ECB rate (e.g. Trade Republic: 2.50%, 4% promo for new customers up to
     EUR 20k until 1 Mar 2027), if you use that broker for re-entry anyway.
  Not crash-safe in the short run: 1-3y bond ETFs (rate risk, -4.29% in 2022), gold (volatile,
  see `haven` lane), Berkshire B (an equity plus USD risk). Fixed-term deposits pay a bit more but
  lock the money, which defeats "buy back in when it pops".
- **If a sale settles in a non-euro currency, convert to euros on the day of the sale (or right
  after settlement), not later.** The krone (NOK), for example, lost ~20% against the euro in
  both the 2008 and 2020 risk-offs; holding a non-euro currency while waiting is the biggest
  avoidable risk in the chain (section 5).
- **Selling at a non-euro broker:** commissions, fund cut-offs, settlement, payout rules and FX
  conversion vary; check your own broker's price list and whether it pays out to a foreign
  account (section 3).

### 2.2 Guarantees and what they cover

- **Dutch deposit guarantee (DGS):** EUR 100,000 per person per bank; statutory payout term
  **7 working days** since 1 Jan 2024 (down from 20 in 2017). Covers savings, current accounts and
  term deposits, not securities. Source: De Nederlandsche Bank (DNB, the Dutch central bank)
  article on the Amsterdam Trade Bank payout
  ([DNB, Jan 2024](https://www.dnb.nl/nieuws-voor-de-sector/nederlandse-depositogarantie-2024/dnb-publiceert-factsheet-over-verloop-dgs-uitkering-amsterdam-trade-bank/)).
  Track record:
  - **Amsterdam Trade Bank**, bankrupt Fri 22 Apr 2022 (Russia sanctions): DGS activated the same
    day, claims portal open from working day 3, compensation available within 7 working days, 95%
    of guaranteed savings paid within one month, EUR 670 million in total (same DNB page).
  - **DSB Bank**, failed Oct 2009 after a bank run: payout took up to **three months** (same DNB
    page; the old regime). Subordinated deposits were not covered
    ([NH Nieuws](https://www.nhnieuws.nl/nieuws/682/geen-garantie-speciale-depositos)).
  - **SNS Reaal**, nationalised 1 Feb 2013 under the Intervention Act: shares and subordinated bonds
    were expropriated, senior debt was spared, and the bank kept running: 1.6 million savings
    accounts and 1 million payment accounts continued and savings (also above EUR 100k) were
    declared safe, so the DGS was never needed
    ([Allen & Overy](https://www.allenovery.com/en-gb/global/news-and-insights/european-finance-litigation-review/northern-europe/expropriation-of-sns-reaal-first-application-of-the-dutch-intervention-act),
    [Radar 2013](https://radar.avrotros.nl/artikel/staat-nationaliseert-sns-voor-37-miljard-11769),
    [Consumentenbond 2013](https://www.consumentenbond.nl/nieuws/2013/zorgen-nationalisatie-sns),
    [government.nl 2023-04-21](https://www.government.nl/latest/news/2023/04/21/ministry-of-finance-to-pay-compensation-to-holders-of-expropriated-sns-securities-and-assets)).
    Expropriated shareholders got nothing; subordinated holders were paid ~EUR 900 million only
    after the Supreme Court ruling of 21 Apr 2023
    ([Accountant.nl 2024](https://www.accountant.nl/nieuws/2024/4/staat-betaalt-900-miljoen-aan-onteigende-sns-reaal-beleggers/)).
    Lesson: deposits are protected, bank shares and bank bonds are not.
  - For any balance below EUR 100k the DGS fully covers the saver at any EU bank; the only cost of
    a failure is up to 7 working days without access (could mean missing the re-entry window).
- **Investor compensation scheme (beleggerscompensatiestelsel):** up to **EUR 20,000** per investor
  if a Dutch broker or bank fails *and* client securities or cash cannot be returned. Client
  securities are held separately from the firm's own assets; market losses are never covered
  ([Rijksoverheid](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/vraag-en-antwoord/financiele-sector/wat-gebeurt-er-met-mijn-tegoeden-als-mijn-beleggingsonderneming-failliet-gaat),
  [DNB BCS](https://www.dnb.nl/voor-de-sector/open-boek-toezicht-sectoren/banken/resolutie/beleggerscompensatiestelsel-bcs/)).
  ETFs, bonds, gold ETCs and BRK.B at a broker fall under this, not under the DGS.
  Cash at DEGIRO is swept into a money-market fund or bank account depending on settings (unverified).

### 2.3 Money-market-type ETFs: mechanics and the 2008 lesson

- **XEON** is not a money-market fund under the EU MMF Regulation; it is a UCITS ETF with an
  **unfunded swap**: the fund holds a basket of securities and swaps their return for the EUR STR
  overnight rate with five banks (Barclays, Deutsche Bank, Goldman Sachs International,
  J.P. Morgan Securities, Societe Generale). Fund size EUR 23.6 bn
  ([justETF](https://www.justetf.com/en/etf-profile.html?isin=LU0290358497), read 2026-11-18).
  If a swap bank fails, the fund's loss is limited to the unpaid swap value, which UCITS caps at
  10% of NAV and which DWS aims to keep under 5%; the fund is then left with the basket, whose
  value can differ from cash in a crash
  ([Trackinsight XEON](https://www.trackinsight.com/en/fund/XEON),
  [DWS overnight-ETF explainer](https://etf.dws.com/en-ch/knowledge/focus-topics/overnight-etfs-an-alternative-to-easy-access-savings-accounts/)).
  Basket composition: (unverified; see DWS factsheet).
- **Amundi CSH** works the same way (synthetic swap on the Solactive EUR Overnight Return index).
- **IEGE / ERNE** hold the bonds physically: no swap counterparty, but a little rate and (ERNE)
  credit risk.
- **The 2008 lesson (Reserve Primary Fund):** a US money fund "broke the buck" on 16 Sep 2008 when
  it wrote off USD 785 million of Lehman paper, NAV USD 0.97, and redemptions were held back for up
  to 7 days ([SEC litigation release LR-21025](https://www.sec.gov/litigation/litreleases/2009/lr21025.htm),
  [Crane Data](https://www.cranedata.com/archives/all-articles/2488)). Two takeaways: (1) a "cash
  fund" is not a deposit, a loss of ~3% and a week's gate can happen exactly in a crash; (2) funds
  holding only government paper or overnight swaps against diversified banks are the low end of
  that risk. A bank deposit under the DGS has neither the NAV risk nor a gate, only the 7-day
  payout delay if the bank fails.

## 3. Selling and moving the money

### 3.1 Sale commissions and settlement

- **Commissions:** at small ticket sizes a broker's per-trade minimum usually binds, not the
  percentage rate, so staged selling multiplies the minimum: each holding times each tranche is a
  separate trade. Some brokers have a cheaper class for small orders, and class changes can take
  effect only after a waiting period; check your own broker's price list a month before staged
  selling. Fund sales are often free of commission (some funds charge).
- **Settlement:** Euronext markets, Oslo Børs included (Euronext Securities Oslo, VPS), settle
  **T+2**: a sale on Monday settles on Wednesday. Some brokers let you withdraw before settlement
  but charge interest. The EU moves to T+1 only on **11 Oct 2027**
  ([ESMA 2026-10-16](https://esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/one-year-go-t1-settlement-regulatory-framework-complete-now-lets-make-it)),
  so T+2 applies all of Dec 2026 (whether non-EU markets on the same timetable follow at once:
  (unverified)). US shares settle T+1.

### 3.2 Getting the cash out of a broker

Selling at a non-euro broker: commissions, fund cut-offs, settlement, payout rules and FX
conversion vary; check your own broker. Points to check in advance: whether it pays out to a
foreign (euro) account at all, or only to a local bank account (which adds a hop); the payout
cut-off time (later requests go the next bank day); whether a withdrawal-delay security feature
is switched on (turn it off in advance); whether a first transfer to a new account triggers manual
checks. Move the proceeds into euros promptly if they settle in another currency.

### 3.3 Currency conversion

Published FX costs range from a few tenths of a percent at specialised transfer services to 1-2%
bank markups, and many banks do not publish their markup on outgoing transfers in a readable form
(unverified per bank). That difference is small next to a single day's move in the currency (the
EUR/NOK fixing, for example, ranged 10.31-11.00 between Aug and Nov 2026, a 6.7% range; section 5).
Receiving SEPA EUR at a Dutch bank is free at the big banks (unverified per bank). A first transfer
through a new service needs identity verification; do it well before December.

## 4. Latest safe fallback sale date

Chain, worked for an Oslo-listed share (the calendar with the most closed days in the table): sell
(T) -> settles T+2 on settlement days -> broker payout (same bank day if before the cut-off, up to
2 bank days) -> conversion and transfer to a euro account (~1 bank day) -> cash in the euro
account. Worst realistic case: 2 + 2 + 1 = 5 bank days after the trade. A broker that pays straight
into a euro account shortens this.

| Trade date | Settles (T+2, skipping 24-27 Dec) | Cash in a euro account by (estimate) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fri 18 Dec | Tue 22 Dec | best Wed 23 Dec, worst Tue 29 Dec | **Comfortable** (2 spare days even in the worst case) |
| Mon 21 Dec | Wed 23 Dec | best Thu 24 Dec, worst Wed 30 Dec | **Latest safe fallback** (31 Dec still spare in the worst case) |
| Tue 22 Dec | Mon 28 Dec | best Tue 29 Dec, worst Thu 31 Dec | Tight: no spare day in the worst case |
| Wed 23 Dec | Tue 29 Dec | Wed 30 - Thu 31 Dec | Risky |
| Mon 28 Dec | Wed 30 Dec | Thu 31 Dec at best, likely Jan | Last sale that certainly settles in 2026; cash likely arrives in 2027 |
| Tue 29 Dec | Thu 31 Dec only if VPS settles that day, else Mon 4 Jan 2027 | 2027 | Do not rely on |
| Wed 30 Dec (last Oslo trading day) | Mon 4 Jan 2027 | 2027 | Trade date in 2026, cash in 2027 |

Reasoning: Oslo is shut on 24 and 31 Dec and its cash leg does not settle on 24-26 Dec, so a sale
after Mon 21 Dec has its settlement pushed past Christmas, into a four-day window (28-31 Dec) where
any delay (bank cut-off times, manual checks on a first transfer to a new foreign account,
transfer-service verification) spills into 2027. The cheapest dry run: send a small amount through
the same route in early December to learn the real duration of each hop. If the goal only needs
the **trade date** in 2026, the absolute last day is Wed 30 Dec; whether trade date or settlement
date counts is outside this lane (no tax research). Recommendation: **plan the main sale earlier
(staged sells in late Nov / early Dec if the playbook says so), with Mon 21 Dec as the last
fallback and Fri 18 Dec preferred.**

## 5. Currency risk over the window (krone example)

- A smaller non-euro currency can weaken sharply in risk-off. The krone (NOK) is the worked
  example; Norges Bank daily fixings
  ([EXR API](https://data.norges-bank.no/api/data/EXR/B.EUR.NOK.SP?format=csv&startPeriod=2020-02-17&endPeriod=2020-04-10&locale=en)):
  - **2020:** EUR/NOK 10.04 on 17 Feb -> **12.3165 on 19 Mar** (+22.7%; the krone lost 18.5% of
    its EUR value in a month) -> 11.51 on 31 Mar. Norges Bank: the krone fell 25% against USD in
    two weeks, an "unprecedented" pace ([Norges Bank staff memo](https://www.norges-bank.no/link/d6c761672e1242db9ecac5658bd0b78d.aspx?ft=.pdf)).
  - **2008:** EUR/NOK 7.96 on 1 Sep -> **9.945 on 29 Dec** (+24.9%; the krone lost ~20%) -> 8.89
    on 31 Mar 2009 ([EXR API](https://data.norges-bank.no/api/data/EXR/B.EUR.NOK.SP?format=csv&startPeriod=2008-08-01&endPeriod=2009-03-31&locale=en)).
  - **2026 so far (Aug-Nov):** range 10.312 (30 Oct) to 11.0003 (6 Aug); 10.6045 on 18 Nov
    ([EXR API](https://data.norges-bank.no/api/data/EXR/B.EUR.NOK.SP?format=csv&startPeriod=2026-08-01&endPeriod=2026-11-18&locale=en)).
- Meaning: a holding on a smaller non-euro market is a double bet for a euro-based investor (stock
  + currency). In a crash both can fall together. Once you decide to sell, **convert the same day**
  (or as soon as the proceeds land) rather than waiting for a "better rate": the asymmetric risk is
  a crash, which is exactly when such a currency drops. Staged selling should mean staged
  converting.
- After conversion, keep the parked money in EUR. BRK.B and gold reintroduce USD exposure (gold is
  priced in USD; the iShares ETC is unhedged).

### 7.2 Fund sales: order cut-off, NAV date, cash

Fund sales go through the fund manager, not the exchange. Across the retail platforms read (broker
FAQs, bank fund pages and fund-company terms), the pattern is:

- **Cut-off:** fund-specific, typically between 06:00 and 13:00 on a business day (earlier on
  half trading days); later orders go the next business day.
- **NAV (price) date:** the same day or 1-2 business days after the order for local funds; up to
  2-4 days for funds investing abroad or held via an external fund platform.
- **Cash:** typically 2-5 business days from the order for local funds and 2-6 for other funds,
  sometimes 3-7; at the slowest platforms 5-10 business days. These are "normally" figures, not
  guarantees.
- **Legal backstop:** national UCITS rules set a maximum; for Oslo-domiciled funds a redemption
  must be settled as soon as possible and at the latest within two weeks of the claim (fund terms
  citing the national fund law). That is the true worst case, not a planning figure.

### 7.3 Dated timeline: last-safe and recommended sale dates

Chain and worst-case assumptions (bank days on the Oslo calendar; 24-27 Dec and, for funds, 31 Dec
skipped; for a holding on another market use that market's calendar):

- Shares: trade T, settles T+2; broker payout worst 2 bank days; transfer to a euro account
  1 day. Worst = T+5.
- Fund with cash in 2-5 days: order D (before the fund's cut-off) -> cash at the broker worst
  D+5 -> payout +2 -> euro account +1. Worst = **D+8**.
- Fund with cash in 2-6 days (global funds): worst = **D+9**.
- Slow platform (5-10 days): cash worst D+10 -> euro account +1. Worst = **D+11**.
- Fund held at a bank (cash lands in the bank account in 2-4 days): worst about D+5.
- "Last safe" = worst case reaches the euro account by **Wed 30 Dec**, keeping Thu 31 Dec as the
  one spare day. "Recommended fallback" = worst case lands by about Wed 23 Dec, before the
  Christmas break, so all of 28-31 Dec is spare.

| Holding type | Recommended fallback | Last-safe day (order in before cut-off) | Worst-case cash in euros if sold on the last-safe day | Worst-case cash in euros if sold on the fallback |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Shares (T+2) | **Fri 18 Dec** | **Mon 21 Dec** (confirmed) | Wed 30 Dec | Tue 29 Dec |
| (b) Fund, cash in 2-5 days | **Fri 11 Dec** | **Wed 16 Dec** | Wed 30 Dec | Wed 23 Dec |
| (c) Global fund, cash in 2-6 days | **Fri 11 Dec** | **Tue 15 Dec** | Wed 30 Dec | Mon 28 Dec |
| Fund at a slow platform (5-10 days) | **Fri 4 Dec** | **Fri 11 Dec** | Wed 30 Dec | Mon 21 Dec |
| Fund held at a bank (2-4 days) | Fri 11 Dec (same rule, for simplicity) | about Mon 21 Dec (D+5) | Wed 30 Dec | well before Christmas |

Worked examples:
- Fund with 2-5 days, order Wed 16 Dec: cash visible by Wed 23 Dec (17, 18, 21, 22, 23); payout
  by Tue 29 Dec (28, 29); euro account Wed 30 Dec.
- Global fund with 2-6 days, order Tue 15 Dec: cash by Wed 23 Dec (16, 17, 18, 21, 22, 23); then
  as above. One day later (Wed 16 Dec) the sixth day is Mon 28 Dec, which some global funds do not
  even deal on, and the chain ends on 31 Dec at best.
- Order Fri 11 Dec: 2-5-day fund cash by Fri 18 Dec, euro account by Wed 23 Dec; global-fund cash
  by Mon 21 Dec, payout by Wed 23 Dec, euro account by Mon 28 Dec (Thu 24 Dec if the transfer
  completes that day).
- A fund order placed after the cut-off on Wed 23 Dec is priced on Mon 28 Dec at the earliest
  (Tue 29 Dec for global funds); its cash will not reach a euro account in 2026.

So the reviewer's **Fri 11 Dec for funds is confirmed** as the recommended fallback (with
step 2's watch window for funds ending Thu 10 Dec), and the last-safe days are Wed 16 Dec
(2-5-day funds) and Tue 15 Dec (2-6-day funds). Slow platforms are the exception: 5-10 days push
the last-safe day to Fri 11 Dec and the fallback to Fri 4 Dec.

Assumptions to state in the report: the "2-5" and "2-6" are counted in business days from the day
the order is sent to the fund manager; payout worst 2 bank days; transfer 1 day; fund NAV and
payout days skip 24, 25, 31 Dec (and 28 Dec for global funds). Platform figures are "normally"
figures, and a fund can take up to the legal two weeks; that is why the fallback, not the
last-safe day, is the plan.

### 7.4 Shares at a bank, and US shares

- **Shares held at a bank broker settle the same way** (T+2 on Euronext markets, Oslo included,
  via [VPS settlement cycle](https://csdclient.vps.no/pub/clearing-and-settlement/settlement-cycle/?lang=en)
  for Oslo, via search result). At a bank the cash then sits in the same bank, which saves the
  broker -> bank hop (worst case T+3 instead of T+5), so the share dates in 7.3 are conservative.
- **US shares:** commissions outside a broker's home market are usually higher (a percentage with
  a higher minimum), and the broker converts the USD proceeds into the account currency at a fee
  (0.25% each way is a common published figure; a currency account can cut it). Keeping USD does
  not help if the broker pays out only in its local currency. Converting USD -> EUR directly is
  cheapest when the broker can pay euros to a euro account.
- **US timing:** US trades settle T+1; withdrawing before settlement can cost overdraft interest.
  NYSE: early close 13:00 ET Thu 24 Dec, closed Fri 25 Dec 2026
  ([NYSE Group 2026-2028 calendar](https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2025/NYSE-Group-Announces-2026-2027-and-2028-Holiday-and-Early-Closings-Calendar/default.aspx)).
  If the payout leg follows a non-euro bank calendar, a US sale is as safe as an Oslo sale one day
  later; keep the Fri 18 Dec / Mon 21 Dec dates for simplicity.

### 7.5 Optional items

- **Thin stocks (e.g. Oslo small caps): order type.** Use a limit order, never a market order.
  Common order types: a plain limit order (rests in the book), "Fill and Kill" (fills what it can
  now, cancels the rest) and "Fill or Kill" (all or nothing); at many brokers neither FaK nor FoK
  works in the opening or closing auction. Practical rule (reasoning, not a tested result): check
  the order book first; if your quantity fits within the volume at the best bid, a limit at the
  best bid sells at once; if not, either sell in two or three slices during the day or place the
  limit one or two ticks below the bid and accept that the spread is the cost. Do not set a limit
  far above the bid on the fallback day: an unfilled order means no sale. Spreads are widest for
  the least liquid shares (Ødegaard, Oslo Børs liquidity talk, 2010,
  [PDF](https://www.ba-odegaard.no/talks/2010_aug_fin/talk.pdf), search snippet); current spread
  levels for small caps: (unverified). Oslo continuous trading runs 09:00-16:20 CET (third-party
  calendars; Euronext's own hours page did not list them), closing-auction details (unverified).
